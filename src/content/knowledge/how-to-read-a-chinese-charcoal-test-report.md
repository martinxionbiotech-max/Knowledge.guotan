# How to Read a Chinese Charcoal Test Report (GB/T 12496 vs ASTM)

A Chinese supplier's test report frequently references "GB/T 12496" and a
sub-number. International buyers who only know ASTM method numbers can miss
what the certificate actually says — or fail to notice what it does not say.

## Quick Answer

GB/T 12496 is the Chinese national standard family for test methods of wooden activated carbon, with part numbers covering density, particle size, ash, moisture, CTC adsorption, strength, pH and iodine value. It was written for activated carbon, not fuel charcoal — so a hookah briquette report citing GB/T 12496.3 (ash) used an activated-carbon-family method rather than the ASTM D1762 or D3172 proximate-analysis family a fuel buyer normally requests. Before accepting any certificate, check the standard and edition, the product class, the reporting basis, the laboratory identity and the sample linkage.

## What GB/T 12496 is

GB/T 12496 is the Chinese national standard family for test methods of wooden
activated carbon — the adsorbent branch of the coconut-shell industry. Public
testing-institution references list the series member methods, including:

| GB/T 12496 part | What it measures |
|---|---|
| 12496.1 | Apparent density |
| 12496.2 | Particle size distribution |
| 12496.3 | Ash content |
| 12496.4 | Moisture content |
| 12496.5 | Carbon tetrachloride (CTC) adsorption activity |
| 12496.6 | Strength |
| 12496.7 | pH value |
| 12496.8 | Iodine adsorption value |

## The key point: which product is the report for?

GB/T 12496 methods were written for **activated carbon**, not for fuel
charcoal. A report citing 12496.3 (ash) attached to a hookah briquette means
the laboratory used an activated-carbon-family method on a fuel product — the
number may still be informative, but it is not the method a hookah buyer would
normally request (ASTM D1762 or the ASTM D3172-75 proximate-analysis family).

## What to check on any Chinese certificate

1. **Which standard, which edition.** "GB/T 12496" alone is incomplete; the
   part number and year edition matter.
2. **Which product class.** Fuel charcoal, carbonized shell or activated
   carbon — the acceptable ranges differ across these classes by an order of
   magnitude in some parameters.
3. **Reporting basis.** Dry basis versus as-received changes moisture and ash
   figures meaningfully.
4. **Laboratory identity.** A report from a named third-party testing
   institution is verifiable; an in-house printout is a supplier statement,
   not an independent result.
5. **Sample linkage.** The report should be traceable to the batch you are
   actually buying — lot number, date and quantity.
