## Quick Answer

Private label charcoal means taking a standard coconut shell product and putting your brand, artwork and packaging specification on it. The product may be identical to what a supplier sells under its own name; what you are buying is the branding, the packaging and the order terms that come with a custom print run — chiefly a higher minimum and a longer lead time.

**Related data & testing:** [Specification database](https://data.chinacharcoalhub.com/) · [Test methodologies](https://testing.chinacharcoalhub.com/)

## Key findings

- **Private label is a packaging and branding exercise** on top of a commodity product, not a different manufacturing process.
- **The binding constraint is usually the print run**, not the charcoal — printed boxes and labels carry their own minimums.
- **MOQ jumps when you add print.** Stock packaging commonly starts around one tonne; printed OEM boxes commonly start at a 20 ft container.
- **Lead time extends to roughly 15–25 days after artwork approval**, because production waits on the print step.
- **The sample comes first.** You approve a pre-production sample before any bulk run or print commitment.

## What private label actually changes

The charcoal is the same product family — coconut shell briquettes in a standard cube size. What changes is everything around it:

| Element | Stock product | Private label |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Branding | Supplier's or neutral | Your logo and colours |
| Packaging | Stock box/bag | Custom printed box + outer carton |
| Minimum order | Lower (~1 tonne) | Higher (often one 20 ft container) |
| Lead time | Shorter | 15–25 days after artwork approval |
| Setup cost | None | Print plates / artwork setup |

## The workflow

1. **Choose product** — coconut shell hookah, BBQ or bamboo charcoal.
2. **Choose size** — 25 / 26 / 27 mm cube, flat, or custom.
3. **Choose packaging** — the retail weight and box format.
4. **Submit artwork** — logo, colours, multilingual panels, barcode, batch coding.
5. **Sample** — approve a pre-production sample before production.
6. **Production** — manufacture to the agreed specification.
7. **Inspection** — check against the agreed spec before shipment.
8. **Shipment** — export to your destination port.

This is the same discipline as [OEM manufacturing](https://chinacharcoalhub.com/oem/) — the sample-first step is what protects you from a container of product that does not match the picture.

## Why MOQ jumps when you add print

Two minimums stack in a private-label order:

1. **The charcoal minimum** — the smallest production and shipping unit that makes economic sense, usually framed in containers.
2. **The print minimum** — the number of printed boxes, labels or cartons a printer will run.

Because the print run often exceeds the charcoal minimum, the effective MOQ rises. This is why public listings commonly describe a one-tonne minimum for stock packaging but a full 20 ft container for printed OEM boxes. The reasoning behind container-based minimums is explained in [coconut charcoal MOQ](/knowledge/coconut-charcoal-moq/).

## Printing options to plan for

- **Logo and colour** on the retail box and outer carton.
- **Multilingual panels** for the destination market's language requirements.
- **Barcode and batch coding** for retail scanning and traceability.
- **Artwork specification** — file format, colour profile and proof approval are your responsibility; a proof error reproduced at scale is expensive to fix.

## Sequencing: validate before you print

The most common and lowest-risk sequence for a new brand is:

1. Order a **sample batch** under neutral or stock packaging.
2. **Test the product** against your own hardware and target market.
3. Only then commit to a **printed run** with your artwork.

Printing before product validation means you own both the product risk and the print risk at once. Sequencing them apart costs a little time and saves a lot of exposure. See [charcoal packaging options](/knowledge/charcoal-packaging-options/).

## Regulatory and labelling considerations

Your branded packaging is your legal face in the destination market. Before you print, confirm with your broker or advisor:

- **Labelling requirements** — language, net weight, and any mandatory warnings or symbols for the destination.
- **Destination compliance** — the EUDR, REACH and packaging rules that may apply to your product and market; see [charcoal import certifications](/knowledge/charcoal-import-certifications/).
- **Accuracy of claims** — any grilling, "natural" or performance claim on your box must be accurate and supportable.

This page is not legal advice; your print run should be reviewed against the destination market's rules before production.

## Conditional recommendations

- **If you are a first-time brand**, validate the product with a neutral sample before any print commitment.
- **If you have an established retail channel**, printed boxes justify their minimum through shelf presentation and repeat ordering.
- **If the print minimum is too high**, negotiate a phased schedule or start with a stickered neutral box.
- **If your market has strict labelling rules**, complete the compliance review before approving artwork.

## Limitations and uncertainty

MOQ, lead time and print options vary by supplier and are reported here as industry practice from public listings, not as any factory's binding terms. The only figures that matter are those in your written quotation, and the artwork approval is the point at which your lead time clock starts.
