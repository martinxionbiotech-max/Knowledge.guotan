# What Drives Coconut Charcoal Prices: The Four Quality Levers

Two suppliers can quote "coconut shell charcoal" at very different prices and
both be honest. The gap is almost always explained by four material levers.
Reading a quote without them is like reading a price without a unit.

## The four quality levers

**1. Moisture.** Water is the cheapest thing in a charcoal bag — and the
heaviest mistake a buyer can pay for. Chinese process references state the
principle plainly: the lower the moisture, the better the value, because you
buy fuel, not freight. A 15% moisture carbonized shell and a 6% moisture
hookah briquette are different products at different price points.

**2. Volatile matter.** The fraction driven off as gas when the material is
heated. Public references tie it directly to utilization rate: the smaller the
volatile fraction, the more of the delivered mass is usable fuel. High volatile
matter also means more smoke and odour at lighting — a visible quality signal
even before any laboratory test.

**3. Ash.** The inert mineral residue. Chinese-language references describe
ash as the single most important factor for adsorption performance in the
activated-carbon branch of the industry, and the same logic holds for fuel:
higher ash means less active surface, less heat and more bowl residue. Ash is
where the raw-material quality shows up first.

**4. Fixed carbon.** Not measured directly — it is what remains after moisture,
volatile matter and ash are subtracted. Fixed carbon is the pure-fuel fraction
and the cleanest single summary of material quality.

## Where the material comes from

Public process references identify Southeast Asia as the main production region
— the Philippines, Indonesia, Malaysia, Thailand, Vietnam and Myanmar — with
the Philippines and Indonesia usually placed first on quality and volume.
Carbonization typically runs at 500–700 °C in continuous furnaces. This
geography matters to price in two ways: shell feedstock cost and freight
distance to your port.

## How to read a quote

For every quote, pin down four numbers in writing: moisture, volatile matter,
ash and fixed carbon — each with its test method and reporting basis. Two
quotes become comparable only when all four are stated. A cheap quote with no
specification is not a price; it is a lottery ticket.
