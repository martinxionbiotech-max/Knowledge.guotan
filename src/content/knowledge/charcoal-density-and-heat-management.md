Density is the charcoal spec nobody quotes and everyone feels. A dense cube
burns longer and steadier; a soft cube crumbles, sparks and finishes early.
Here is how density connects to the numbers that do appear on datasheets.

## Quick Answer

Pressed shisha charcoal is carbonized shell powder plus a small share of binder, compacted under pressure, and public process references place its working density around 1.2-1.3 tonnes per cubic metre with cube thickness from 8 mm to 30 mm. Higher density means more mass in the same volume, so a dense cube burns longer and steadier, survives handling, and heats more evenly. Density is independent of ash content, and absorbed water adds weight without adding fuel density. A buyer should ask for pressing pressure or measured density and a drop-test result, then confirm by hand.

## What density means for a pressed briquette

Pressed hookah charcoal is carbonized shell powder plus a small share of
binder, compacted under high pressure. Public process references place the
working density of pressed shisha briquettes around 1.2-1.3 tonnes per cubic
metre, with cube thickness from 8 mm to 30 mm. Density matters in three ways:

1. **Burn duration.** More mass in the same volume means a longer, steadier
   heat release. A 25 mm cube burning roughly 60 minutes and a soft cube
   finishing much sooner are often the same size and different density.
2. **Mechanical integrity.** Dense cubes survive handling and stacking; weak
   cubes arrive as dust. Suppliers test this with a drop test — premium grades
   advertise surviving repeated drops.
3. **Heat stability.** A dense, uniform cube heats evenly; voids and density
   variation inside the cube create hot spots and uneven sessions.

## How density relates to the other specs

| Spec | Relationship to density |
|---|---|
| Burn time | Higher density lengthens it for the same cube size |
| Ash | Independent of density — ash comes from the raw material's mineral content |
| Moisture | Absorbed water adds weight, not density of carbon — a wet cube is heavier but not denser in fuel terms |
| Fixed carbon | The fuel fraction inside the dense body; density decides how much of it fits in the cube |

## What a buyer should ask

1. Pressing pressure or measured briquette density from the supplier's QC
   sheet — most can state it even when they do not advertise it.
2. Drop-test results for the specific size you are buying.
3. A sample: density differences are obvious in hand within seconds (weight,
   hardness, how a cube breaks), and a 2 kg sample is cheap insurance against
   buying a season of bad sessions.
