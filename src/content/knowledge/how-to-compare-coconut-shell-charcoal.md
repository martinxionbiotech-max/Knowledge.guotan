## Quick Answer

Compare coconut shell charcoal on a fixed set of measurable properties — fixed carbon, ash, moisture, volatile matter, bulk density, size tolerance and impurities — and only accept values that state the test method and laboratory. A specification is only comparable when the method and reporting basis match. Batch consistency matters more than a single best value. Coconut shell charcoal is typically low-ash relative to lump wood charcoal, but "typically" is not an enforceable number — specify a maximum.

**Related data & testing:** [Fixed carbon specification](https://data.guotan.com/specifications/fixed-carbon/) · [Fixed carbon test method](https://testing.guotan.com/tests/fixed-carbon/)

## Key findings

- **A specification is only comparable when the method is stated.** Ash measured by ASTM D1762 is not directly comparable to an in-house gravimetric method.
- **Six properties carry most of the buying decision:** fixed carbon, ash, moisture, volatile matter, bulk density and size tolerance.
- **Batch consistency matters more than a single best value.** A supplier whose ash is consistently 3% beats one that alternates between 2% and 6%.
- **Coconut shell charcoal is typically low-ash** relative to lump wood charcoal, but "typically" is not a number you can enforce — specify a maximum.
- **Ask for the raw laboratory report,** not a summary PDF, and confirm the lab is independent of the supplier.

## What a coconut shell charcoal spec sheet should contain

A usable spec sheet names the property, the value, the unit, the test method and the laboratory. If any of those is missing, the value cannot be compared to another supplier's.

| Property | What it measures | Why it matters to a buyer | Typical public range* |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Fixed carbon | Combustible carbon fraction | Heat and burn duration | ~70–80%+ |
| Ash | Non-combustible residue | Residue, airflow, emissions | ~2–5% |
| Moisture | Water content | Ignition, weight loss, mould risk | ≤10% |
| Volatile matter | Volatiles released on heating | Smoke, odour, emissions | ~10–18% |
| Bulk density | Mass per unit volume | Container loading economics | varies by form |
| Size / tolerance | Cube dimensions and variation | Bowl fit, stacking | stated ± tolerance |
| Impurities | Sand, stone, foreign matter | Integrity, complaints | low / stated |

*Ranges are compiled from published supplier specifications and peer-reviewed characterisation studies. They are illustrative, not a standard. See the Sources table.

## How to read the sheet line by line

### Fixed carbon

Fixed carbon is the fraction of the material that burns as carbon. Higher fixed carbon generally correlates with more heat and longer burn, and the European Commission's review linked higher fixed carbon with lower emissions in testing. Treat the number as a range and confirm it with a lab report.

### Ash

Ash is what is left when everything combustible is gone. For barbecue charcoal standards, EN 1860-2 sets a maximum ash of 8% for lump charcoal and 18% for briquettes — values that reveal how permissive a briquette specification can be. If a supplier quotes far below these, ask for the method. See [what ash content means](/knowledge/what-ash-content-means/).

### Moisture and volatile matter

Moisture adds weight you pay for and can drive mould in transit. Volatile matter relates to how much smoke and odour the product releases early in a session. Both are proximate-analysis values and both should be measured under a stated method such as ASTM D1762.

### Bulk density and size tolerance

Bulk density decides how many tonnes fit in a container, which decides freight per tonne. Size tolerance decides whether a cube actually fits a heat-management device. A cube "25 mm" without a stated tolerance is an unfinished specification. See [25 mm vs 26 mm charcoal](/knowledge/25mm-vs-26mm-charcoal/).

## How to request a spec sheet that you can actually use

1. Ask for the property list **plus method plus laboratory** in one table.
2. Request the **raw third-party report** for the most recent production batch.
3. Ask for **three consecutive batches** to judge consistency, not one best sample.
4. Ask which properties are **guaranteed on the contract** versus "typical".
5. Ask how the supplier handles a **failed arrival test** (replacement, credit, rework).

## Batch consistency: the hidden variable

Two suppliers can both declare "ash max 3%" and behave completely differently. Consistency is a variance question: you want the spread, not the headline. Ask for control-chart style data across batches, or test three shipments yourself and compare. A supplier who cannot produce batch history is a risk even if the single spec sheet looks perfect.

## Conditional recommendations

- **If you are qualifying a new supplier**, insist on three-batch history and independent reports before placing a full container order.
- **If you are optimising for a regulated EU barbecue market**, anchor your minimum specification to EN 1860-2 limits and require declaration-of-conformity documentation where applicable.
- **If you are buying for a hookah brand**, weight size tolerance and odour over marginal ash differences; session feel is usually decided by geometry and purity.
- **If you are a trader reselling without repacking**, weight moisture and packaging integrity above fixed carbon, because storage damage costs more than a few points of carbon.

## Limitations and uncertainty

There is no single international specification for hookah charcoal; EN 1860-2 addresses barbecue charcoal and briquettes, and ASTM D1762 provides analysis methods rather than acceptance limits. Values in the table are illustrative ranges assembled from public sources and should not be quoted as a standard. Supplier claims must be re-verified per shipment.
