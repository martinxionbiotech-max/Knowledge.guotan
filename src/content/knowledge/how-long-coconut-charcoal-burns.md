**Direct answer:** There is no universal burn time for coconut charcoal. Published figures commonly land in a broad band — roughly 60 to 120 minutes for a 25 mm cube depending on conditions — because burn time is decided by density, cube size, airflow, hardware, bowl design and draw, not by a single product property.

## Key findings

- **Burn time is emergent, not intrinsic.** It is the output of a combustion system, so two users with the same charcoal can get very different results.
- **Mass and density dominate.** A larger, denser cube carries more carbon and lasts longer, all else equal.
- **Airflow cuts both ways.** More airflow means more heat but faster consumption; heat-management devices and bowl design change the curve.
- **Public claims vary widely.** Sources report ranges from around 60 minutes to over 120 minutes for similar products — a sign that test conditions differ, not that one source is simply correct.
- **Ask for conditions, not minutes.** A burn-time claim without a described test setup is not comparable.

## Why there is no single number

Charcoal combustion is governed by how fast oxygen reaches the carbon surface and how fast heat is carried away. Those are system properties. Change the bowl, the number of coals, the wind, or the device, and the burn time changes — with identical charcoal.

That is why a supplier's "burn time" figure is really a claim about a scenario. If the scenario is not described, the figure cannot be verified or compared. Treat it as a marketing input.

## The factors that move burn time

| Factor | Effect on burn time | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cube size / mass | Larger → longer | More carbon mass to consume |
| Density / fixed carbon | Higher → longer | More burnable material per unit volume |
| Ash content | Higher → can shorten usable heat | Ash layer restricts oxygen flow |
| Moisture | Higher → shorter, erratic | Energy lost to evaporating water |
| Airflow / device | Higher → faster consumption | Faster oxygen supply |
| Number of coals | More coals → different curve | Heat load and management strategy |
| Ambient conditions | Wind, humidity change results | Heat loss and moisture uptake |

For the underlying metrics, see [what ash content means](/knowledge/what-ash-content-means/) and [how to compare coconut shell charcoal](/knowledge/how-to-compare-coconut-shell-charcoal/).

| Source type | Product | Reported burn time |

| Product data | 25 mm hookah cube | ~90–120 min |
| Community reports | 25 mm coconut cube | ~60 min usable |

These do not agree, and that is the point: the ranges reflect different products and, more importantly, different test setups. A buyer should reproduce the test that matters for their own use case.

## How to compare burn-time claims fairly

1. **Fix the setup.** Same bowl, same number of coals, same device, same draw pattern.
2. **Blind the samples.** Remove branding so the test is about performance.
3. **Measure to a defined endpoint.** "Until unusable in session" is a different endpoint from "until the coal self-extinguishes".
4. **Repeat at least three times.** Combustion is variable; one run proves nothing.
5. **Record ambient conditions.** Wind and humidity move the numbers.

## What to ask a supplier instead of "how long does it burn?"

- What is the **fixed carbon** and **moisture** content, and by what method?
- What is the **cube mass** (g) and **size tolerance**?
- Under what **test conditions** was the burn-time claim measured?
- Can you supply a **sample batch** for our own controlled test?

Put the answers into the [specification data hub](https://data.chinacharcoalhub.com/specifications/burn-time/) and compare them consistently.

## Conditional recommendations

- **If you run a lounge**, measure session-length reliability across a full shift, not a single burn, and standardise your own device setup.
- **If you distribute**, prefer consistent burn over the longest claim, because variance drives complaints.
- **If you are comparing suppliers**, run one identical test protocol and compare like for like.
- **If a marketing claim is far above the public band**, ask for the test method before assuming the product is exceptional.

## Limitations and uncertainty

All figures here are reported ranges from public sources and were not independently measured for this article. Combustion performance depends on the user's system. Public sources disagree, and where they do, this article preserves the disagreement rather than picking a number.
